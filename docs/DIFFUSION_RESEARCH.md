# Diffusion-Based Time-Series Modeling — Research Review

**Status:** Phase 5-6 deliverable. This document reviews candidate diffusion
architectures against the constraints established in
[DATA_PIPELINE.md](DATA_PIPELINE.md) (3 channels, irregular sampling, real
missingness, short/unfixed sequence length, no session ID, no real training
data yet). It does not select a final architecture — that reasoning is in
[MODEL_SELECTION.md](MODEL_SELECTION.md).

---

## 1. Comparison table

| Model | Primary task | Architecture | Conditioning | Healthcare relevance | Pros | Cons | Relevance to HealthPulse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CSDI** ([Tashiro et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03502), [official repo](https://github.com/ermongroup/CSDI)) | Probabilistic imputation (also usable for forecasting by masking the future) | Score-based (DDPM-style) diffusion over a 2D (time × feature) grid; two-stage transformer attention (temporal attention across time, feature attention across channels) as the denoising network | **Conditions directly on observed values via a binary mask** at every diffusion step — the network sees observed values + a mask + noised unobserved values | Evaluated by the original authors on healthcare time series (PhysioNet mortality/ICU dataset) with missing values — closest published precedent to HealthPulse's actual data shape | Native missing-value handling; one model does imputation and (via a masking trick) short-horizon forecasting; well-documented reference implementation; works at small channel counts | Attention over the full time×feature grid scales worse for very long sequences (not a concern at HealthPulse's short windows); sampling requires the full reverse diffusion chain (tens of steps) per request, so inference latency must be budgeted | **High.** The mask-conditioning mechanism maps almost 1:1 onto HealthPulse's actual missingness (timestamp gaps / null fields from `fingerPresent=false`). 3 channels is small enough that the feature-attention block is cheap. |
| **Diffusion-TS** ([Yuan & Qiao, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01742), [official repo](https://github.com/Y-debug-sys/Diffusion-TS)) | General time-series generation (also supports forecasting/imputation via conditional sampling) | Transformer encoder-decoder with explicit trend/seasonal decomposition (inspired by classical time-series decomposition), denoises in a way that targets **direct reconstruction of x0** rather than noise prediction, plus an auxiliary Fourier-domain loss to preserve frequency content | Conditional variants support masking for imputation/forecasting, but the architecture is built primarily as an unconditional/semi-conditional generator | Applied in the paper to general benchmarks (energy, stocks, ETT, etc.), not specifically healthcare, though nothing prevents it | Strong synthetic-generation fidelity (autocorrelation/frequency structure preserved via the Fourier loss); a single architecture nominally covers generation + forecasting + imputation | Meaningfully heavier: trend/seasonal decomposition and Fourier loss are designed for longer, more structured series (e.g., daily/weekly seasonality) than HealthPulse's short, noisy, session-scale windows; larger transformer footprint increases both training-data appetite and CPU inference latency; decomposition assumptions (trend + seasonality) are a poor fit for vitals data that is closer to a noisy near-stationary process over a few minutes than a seasonal signal | **Medium.** The unified generation/forecast/imputation framing is attractive, but the added architectural complexity (decomposition + Fourier loss) is not justified by HealthPulse's short windows and currently-synthetic-only training data. Worth revisiting if/when longer multi-hour or multi-day series with real seasonality (e.g., circadian HR patterns) become available. |
| **TimeGrad** ([Rasul et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12072), reference implementations exist in the GluonTS ecosystem) | Autoregressive probabilistic **forecasting** only | RNN (LSTM/GRU) encodes history into a hidden state at each step; a conditional diffusion model (DDPM) denoises the *next* timestep's value conditioned on that hidden state; repeated autoregressively for the full forecast horizon | Conditions on the RNN's hidden state (i.e., all prior observed history), not on an explicit mask — not designed for imputation of gaps *within* the observed window | Applied to multivariate forecasting benchmarks (traffic, electricity, solar); not healthcare-specific, and its autoregressive-diffusion design was later noted (by follow-up literature) to be slow at inference because a full diffusion sampling chain runs at *every* forecast step | Genuinely uncertainty-aware, autoregressive forecasts; conceptually simple to reason about (RNN + diffusion head) | **Forecasting-only** — cannot naturally do imputation of gaps inside the observed context, which HealthPulse's data has today; inference latency compounds badly (diffusion steps × forecast horizon steps), a real concern for a dashboard that should feel responsive; RNN backbone is generally harder to parallelize/train than attention-based alternatives at small scale | **Low-medium.** Forecasting is a real secondary task for HealthPulse, but TimeGrad's design doesn't address the imputation need at all, and its per-step diffusion sampling cost is a poor fit for a low-latency dashboard feature. |
| **PriSTI** ([Liu et al., ICDE 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09746)) | Spatiotemporal imputation (originally for sensor networks / traffic / air quality with spatial correlation across sensors) | Conditional diffusion with an explicit spatiotemporal attention mechanism and a coarse interpolation step used as extra conditioning signal | Conditions on observed values + a learned coarse interpolation, plus spatial graph structure across sensors | Not healthcare-specific; the "spatial" dimension (correlated sensor *locations*) has no analogue in HealthPulse, which has a single wearable per user | The coarse-interpolation-as-conditioning idea is a genuinely useful trick, reusable independent of the spatial-graph machinery | The core architectural contribution (spatial graph attention across sensor locations) is irrelevant to a single-wearable, 3-channel setting — adopting the full model would import unused complexity | **Low.** No spatial/multi-sensor structure exists in HealthPulse today. Only the coarse-interpolation-as-conditioning idea is portable, and CSDI-style mask conditioning already covers the core need more simply. |
| **SAITS** ([Du et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08516)) | Deterministic (non-diffusion) multivariate time-series imputation | Self-attention-based, dual diagonally-masked attention blocks, trained with a joint reconstruction + imputation loss | Mask-based conditioning, similar spirit to CSDI but **not diffusion** — outputs a single point estimate, not a distribution | General-purpose; frequently used as a strong deterministic imputation baseline in healthcare time-series papers | Fast (single forward pass, no iterative sampling), simple to train and serve, strong empirical accuracy for point imputation | No uncertainty/probabilistic output — cannot express "82 ± uncertainty," which is one of the stated goals (probabilistic prediction) | **Medium, as a baseline, not as the production model.** SAITS is exactly the right shape for the *mandatory non-diffusion imputation baseline* required by the implementation plan (comparable in spirit to CSDI's own paper, which also benchmarks against deterministic imputers). It is not being selected as the primary architecture because it cannot produce the probabilistic/uncertainty output that is one of HealthPulse's stated goals. |
| **TimeDiff** (referenced in recent time-series-diffusion survey literature as a non-autoregressive forecasting diffusion model) | Forecasting, with a future-mixup / autoregressive-initialization conditioning trick to reduce sampling steps | Non-autoregressive: predicts the entire forecast window in one diffusion pass rather than step-by-step like TimeGrad | Conditions on a coarse linear/autoregressive initialization of the future window, refined via diffusion | Not healthcare-specific | Addresses TimeGrad's main weakness (per-step sampling cost) by forecasting the whole horizon at once | Narrower scope than CSDI (forecasting-only, no native imputation); as a newer/less standardized architecture, fewer mature reference implementations exist to adapt from safely | **Low-medium.** The "non-autoregressive whole-window" idea is worth borrowing conceptually (it's compatible with CSDI's own sampling scheme when the "future" is simply treated as fully-masked positions), but does not add anything CSDI-style masking doesn't already offer for HealthPulse's short windows. |
| **Latent-space diffusion for imputation** (general pattern seen across several 2023-2024 time-series-diffusion papers, e.g. latent CSDI variants) | Imputation/generation with diffusion run in a learned low-dimensional latent space instead of raw value space | An autoencoder (or VAE) compresses the window to a latent representation; diffusion operates on the latent; a decoder reconstructs raw values | Same mask-based conditioning ideas as CSDI, applied in latent space | Not healthcare-specific as a category; motivated mainly by scaling diffusion to long/high-dimensional series | Cheaper diffusion sampling once the latent space is small; can help when raw dimensionality is large | Adds an entire extra model (the autoencoder) to train, validate, and keep in sync with the diffusion model; the latent space itself becomes another source of information loss and calibration risk | **Low.** HealthPulse's raw dimensionality is already tiny (3 channels × a short window) — the entire motivation for latent-space diffusion (reducing a large raw space) doesn't apply. Adding an autoencoder here would be pure unjustified complexity. |

## 2. What is actually reusable from each, independent of final selection

- **From CSDI:** the mask-conditioning mechanism (concatenate observed values, an observation mask, and the noised target, then let attention mix across time and features) is the single most transferable idea for HealthPulse — it directly matches the real missingness pattern found in [DATA_PIPELINE.md](DATA_PIPELINE.md) §5, and it naturally unifies imputation and short-horizon forecasting (forecasting is just "the mask says the near future is unobserved").
- **From Diffusion-TS:** the idea of an auxiliary frequency-domain (FFT/Fourier) loss term is a cheap, architecture-agnostic addition that can improve how physiologically-plausible generated waveforms look (e.g., preserving a sensible heart-rate variability spectrum) without adopting the full trend/seasonal-decomposition transformer.
- **From TimeGrad:** the framing of forecasting as "diffuse the next unobserved value(s) conditioned on history" is useful even outside an RNN — it reinforces that forecasting and imputation are the same underlying operation (predict masked positions) rather than two separate models.
- **From PriSTI:** using a cheap deterministic interpolation (e.g., linear interpolation across the gap) as an *extra* conditioning input alongside the raw observed values is a low-cost accuracy improvement that doesn't require adopting spatial graph attention.
- **From SAITS:** its self-attention/masking design is directly usable, unmodified in spirit, as the **non-diffusion imputation baseline** mandated by the implementation plan (Phase 14) — diffusion must be shown to beat a real deterministic baseline, not evaluated in isolation.
- **From TimeDiff / non-autoregressive forecasting diffusion:** confirms that a single denoising pass over the whole future window (rather than TimeGrad's step-by-step RNN loop) is both a known, validated pattern and directly compatible with CSDI-style masking — i.e., HealthPulse does not need to choose between "CSDI-style" and "non-autoregressive forecasting" as competing paradigms, the latter falls out of the former for free by masking the whole future window at once.
- **From latent-space diffusion:** confirms, by contrast, that this direction is *not* worth pursuing at HealthPulse's current data dimensionality — useful mainly as a documented reason to not add an autoencoder later without a concrete scaling motivation (e.g., adding many more channels).

## 3. Explicitly out of scope for this review

Full training runs of any of the above official repositories were **not** performed as part of this research phase — no benchmark numbers are quoted from independently reproducing these papers on HealthPulse data (none exists yet), only what each paper/repo reports about its own published benchmarks, cited above. Any performance claims made later in this project must be clearly labeled as either "reported by the original paper," "smoke-test only," or "measured by us on synthetic/real HealthPulse data" — never blended (see the philosophy note in the implementation plan about not fabricating results).
